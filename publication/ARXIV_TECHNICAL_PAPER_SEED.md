# arXiv technical paper seed

## Working title
Pure Logic: A Reality-First Architecture for Recursively Correctable Artificial Intelligence

## Abstract
Pure Logic is a proposed reality-first reasoning architecture in which no internal representation receives permanent authority. Observations, models, objectives, verifiers, memories, logic systems, and architectural components remain provisional, scoped, testable, and revocable. The framework is organized around six Master Laws: Reality, Survival, Investigation, Plurality, Path, and Resource. These laws are operationalized through mechanisms including provenance-aware evidence records, contradiction memory, scope-limited authority, dependency graphs, recursive authority revocation, verifier independence tracking, active reality probing, objective revision, execution gates, Zero State, and recursive verification of self-modification. Zero AI is the machine architecture intended to implement these mechanisms across perception, memory, planning, strategy, experimentation, and long-term learning. This paper should evaluate the architecture through explicit failure cases and quantitative benchmarks rather than philosophical assertion alone, including authority escape rate, detection time, revocation time, blast radius, false action rate, false hold rate, recovery time, attack recurrence rate, scope-certification failures, and adversarial tests targeting compromised evidence and verifiers. The central research question is whether a recursively correctable architecture can preserve useful capability while preventing unsupported internal authority from silently propagating into high-consequence execution.

## Core claims to test
1. Discovery confidence must not automatically grant scope authority.
2. Authority must propagate through explicit dependency structures and be revocable when upstream evidence fails.
3. Verifier agreement should increase authority only in proportion to genuine independence.
4. Contradiction should trigger causal localization, preserved failure memory, repair, and stronger retesting.
5. Reasoning authority and execution authority should remain separate.
6. High-consequence actions should require stronger evidence, lower unresolved contradiction, and acceptable irreversibility.
7. Recursive capability improvement should not outrun independent verification and rollback capacity.

## Empirical section to build next
Use Zero-OS benchmarks and adversarial attack-lab results to report quantitative outcomes. The paper should distinguish demonstrated results from proposed architecture and should keep claims limited to tested scope.
